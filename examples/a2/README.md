# A₂ Boussinesq: equation-only quasi-Miura map and DZ loop check

## Normalization and exact input

Use the `κ=3` normalization of [Dubrovin–Zhang (3.6.55), PDF p. 94](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0108160#page=94):
the flat potential is `V² U/2 + U⁴/8` and the flat metric interchanges
the two coordinates. The `t^(2,0)` flow is the Boussinesq flow
([PDF p. 168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0108160#page=168)):

```text
U_t = D_x V,
V_t = 3 U D_x U + (ε²/4) D_x³ U.
```

To fix the dispersion convention, use the Poisson-pencil correction on
[PDF p. 167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0108160#page=167). At `κ=3` the
first-bracket coordinate changes in (3.10.125) vanish. The displayed
`ε²` vector has first component `U_x²/3 + 5 U U_xx/12`. Its integral
has variational derivative `U_xx/6` with respect to `U`. Hence the
correction to the second-bracket vector generated by `∫V dx` is
`(U_xxx/6,0)` in `(V,U)` order. The bihamiltonian recursion multiplies
this by `3/2` for the primary `t^(2,0)` flow.

The input really terminates at ε²: the cubic Gelfand–Dickey Lax
operator `L=𝔇³+A 𝔇+B` with `𝔇=σ ε D_x` satisfies
`L_t=[𝔇²+2A/3,L]/(σ ε)`. For `C=B−σ ε A_x/2`, its two equations are
`A_t=2 C_x` and `C_t=−(2/3) A A_x−(σ² ε²/6) A_xxx`.
Set `σ²=−3/4`, `A=−9U/4`, `C=−9V/8` to obtain the real flow above.
The imaginary root is just a Lax convention, not a change in the
real canonical domain of the symbolic benchmark.

## Construct the map from the flow

Put `U=3 b²`, `r,s=V±6 b³`, so `b=((r−s)/12)^(1/3)`.
The real computation uses `b>0` and nonzero `r_x,s_x`. Its exact
Laurent identities also make sense formally away from the stated
denominators. In Riemann coordinates,

```text
X₀ = (3 b r_x, −3 b s_x),
X₂ = (D_x³(3 b²), D_x³(3 b²))/4,
X₄ = X₆ = 0.
```

The higher pure derivatives of the speeds are nonzero. In the
normalized hodograph transport the translation term acting on the
inverse jet `z_(i,n)` is `−∂_(r^i)^n v^i` at **every** positive order
`n`, not just `n=1` as for the linear Toda speeds. The state
derivatives are `∂r=(36 b²)^−1 ∂b`, `∂s=−(36 b²)^−1 ∂b` on
translation-invariant state coefficients. At each order the solver
integrates the forced highest transverse jet and then descends the
own-jet powers. It does not solve for a guessed ansatz.

`compute.py` constructs `G₂,G₄,G₆` from the input and verifies their
physical homological brackets. It then checks the reducing map directly
on the diagonal flow and the inverse dressing map directly on the flat
Boussinesq equations through `O(ε⁷)`. The coefficient counts per
canonical component at orders 2 / 4 / 6 are 6 / 107 / 880 for the
generator, 6 / 110 / 844 for reduction and 6 / 115 / 872 for dressing.

## Extract the free energies before using the circle equation

The flat tau-gauge corrections `U_(2g), V_(2g)` in the dress map give
`D_x² F_g=U_(2g)`, `D_x E₀ F_g=V_(2g)`, where `E₀` evolves along the
dispersionless flow. The implementation twice integrates the first
relation and verifies the second, as well as the tau-form PDE.
No published free-energy coefficient is used in either the construction
or the primitive. Choosing irrelevant constants to be zero gives:

- `F₁ = (log|r_x|+log|s_x|)/24`.
- `F₂`: 32 terms, jet order 4. Its entire order-four jet part is
  `(b/192) (r_xxxx/r_x² − s_xxxx/s_x²)`.
- `F₃`: 290 terms, jet order 7. Its entire order-seven jet part is
  `(b²/2304) (D_x⁷r/r_x³ + D_x⁷s/s_x³)`.

The complete expressions are in `a2-epsilon6.json` with exact rational
coefficients and an index-to-symbol legend. The free-energy keys are
`2,4,6`, i.e. their associated map orders, not their loop orders.
The genus-two density also agrees exactly with the independent
A₂ genus-two topological recursion formula in [Eguchi–Getzler–Xiong (2.5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0007194#page=7).

## Independent DZ equation

For the period equation [DZ (3.10.95), PDF p. 150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0108160#page=150),
use `a=V−λ`, `Δ=a²−36 b⁶=(r−λ)(s−λ)`, with spectral parameter λ
held constant in every total derivative. The roots of
`z³/9−U z+a=0` are flat periods, with two independent combinations.
Their Gram contraction is `(1/3)Σ` over the three roots. Contracting the
period covectors gives the inverse intersection metric
`[[-2 b²,a],[a,−18 b⁴]]/Δ`; the implementation iterates its
Levi-Civita connection without selecting any cubic branch.
The Euler resolvent has components `(a,−2 b²)/Δ`.

The contracted period pairing is
`−2(a²+45 b⁶)/(9 Δ²) + 2/(9 λ²)` in the DZ normalization at spectral
infinity. This can be derived by differentiating the implicit periods
and integrating the contracted derivatives in the flat variables.
After cancellation with `κ₀/λ²`, where `κ₀=1/9`, the genus-one
source is `−(a²+45 b⁶)/(9 Δ²)`. This calculation uses the period
form directly, not the trace-sign shorthand in the later formula.

For `ΔF=F₁+ε² F₂+ε⁴ F₃+O(ε⁶)`, the checker differentiates in the flat
jets by the exact chain rule and verifies the coefficients of ε⁰, ε²,
and ε⁴. Multiplying their respective residuals by `Δ², Δ⁵, Δ⁸` gives
identically zero Laurent numerators in independent jets and `a`.
Changing a highest-jet coefficient of `F₃` produces a nonzero residual
at ε⁴ without changing either lower order.

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```sh
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python -m examples.a2.compute -o /tmp/a2-epsilon6.json
cmp /tmp/a2-epsilon6.json examples/a2/a2-epsilon6.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python -m examples.a2.loop_equation /tmp/a2-epsilon6.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python -m examples.a2.potentials /tmp/a2-epsilon6.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python -m unittest tests.test_a2 -v
```

All statements are formal and finite-order, not a claim about convergence
or genera above three.
